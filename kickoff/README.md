# Kickoff

The earliest known use of the term _business intelligence_ is in Richard Millar Devens' _Cyclopædia of Commercial and Business Anecdotes_ (1865). Devens used the term to describe how the banker [Sir Henry Furnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Henry_Furnese,_1st_Baronet) gained profit by receiving and acting upon information about his environment, prior to his competitors:

> Throughout Holland, Flanders, France, and Germany, he maintained a complete and perfect train of business intelligence. The news of the many battles fought was thus received first by him, and the [fall of Namur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Namur_\(1695\)) added to his profits, owing to his early receipt of the news.

— Devens, p. 210

The ability to collect and react accordingly based on the information retrieved, Devens says, is central to business intelligence.
